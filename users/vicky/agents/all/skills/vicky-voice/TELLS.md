# The tell list

The sweep in step 4 walks this list top to bottom. Each entry gives the tell
and the fix. Her habits in [`VOICE.md`](VOICE.md) outrank every entry here.

## Words and phrasing

- **Puffery** — *vibrant, rich tapestry, groundbreaking, cutting-edge,
  seamless, robust, holistic, intricate, multifaceted, dynamic, transformative,
  game-changer, pivotal, crucial, delve, dive into, underscore, testament,
  beacon, landscape, realm, journey, roadmap, myriad, plethora, leverage,
  harness, foster, unleash, unlock, empower, streamline, navigate, showcase,
  boasts, nestled, in the heart of, meticulous, comprehensive, paradigm shift,
  scalable*. Say the concrete thing instead: what it does, how fast, for whom.
- **Significance talk** — *marks a pivotal moment, plays a vital role in,
  stands as a key part of, sets the stage for, leaves an indelible mark, shed
  light on, pave the way, this is where X really shines, the key takeaway is,
  it cannot be overstated*. State the fact and let the reader judge its size.
- **Flattened judgment** — *this ultimately led to the conclusion that the two
  served similar purposes*. If the author has a view, write the view:
  *prestige classes were just subclasses wearing a fancy hat*.
- **Participle tails** — a clause hung on the end starting with *highlighting,
  underscoring, showcasing, reflecting, ensuring, contributing to, fostering*.
  Cut it, or promote it to its own sentence with real content in it.
- **Negative parallelism** — *not just X, but Y*; *it isn't X — it's Y*; *this
  isn't about X, it's about Y*; *no X, no Y, just Z*. Say Y.
- **The rule of three** — *fast, simple, and reliable*. Keep the item that
  matters. Vary list lengths across the piece; two and four are allowed.
- **Dodging "is"** — *serves as, functions as, stands as, acts as, represents,
  boasts, features*. Use *is*, *are*, *has*.
- **Elegant variation** — swapping in a synonym each time the same thing comes
  up (*constraint*, then *obstacle*, then *confine*). Repeat the one right word;
  repetition reads as precision in technical prose. The author's own odd word
  counts double: keep *crucible* if that is what they say, even where *test rig*
  would be tidier.
- **Vague sourcing** — *experts argue, studies show, observers note, it is
  widely regarded, most people, a lot of folks, in my experience most teams*.
  Say who, or cut the claim. Never invent the people.
- **Vague links** — *in connection with, associated with, in relation to*. Say
  what the link is: *caused by*, *built on*, *replaced*.
- **Hedge stacks** — *may potentially help in some cases*, *it could be argued
  that*, *generally speaking*, *while it is true that*. Keep one qualifier, or
  commit. An open question in plain words is not a hedge: *I don't know yet
  why the second run is slower* says something and stays.
- **Sweep constructions** — *whether it's X or Y*, *from X to Y*, *ranging
  from X to Y*. Give the two or three real examples that matter.

## Claude's own metaphors

Claude has a private stock of figurative verbs and nouns. Each one sounds
thoughtful and says nothing a plain verb would not. Vicky spots them on sight.
Swap each for the literal word.

- **Shape** — *the shape of the problem*, *a fix of that shape*, *the same
  shape*. Say *kind*, *form*, *layout*, or describe the thing.
- **Names** — *this names the problem*, *the named risk*, *naming it*. Write
  *says*, *calls*, *is*, *describes*, or drop it: *the risk is X*.
- **Lives** — *the bug lives in the parser*, *the tension lives in the gap*.
  Write *is in*.
- **Carries, holds, hands** — *that carries two costs*, *the line it holds*,
  *what the model hands back*. Write *has*, *keeps*, *returns*, *gives*.
- **Doing the work** — *the word is doing the work*, *doing the heavy
  lifting*, *load-bearing*, *the engine of*. Say which part matters and why.
- **Lands, hits** — *this lands*, *hits hardest*, *struck a chord*, *stuck
  with me*, *the part that got me*. Say what it changed or who it affects.
- **Points, moves, turns** — *the evidence points toward*, *the argument moves
  to*, *it all turns on trust*, *what moved is*. Write *shows*, *depends on*,
  *what changed is* only if a fact follows at once.
- **Physics, surface, throughline** — *different physics*, *surface a
  pattern*, *the throughline*. Write *rules*, *find* or *show*, *common
  thread* or the thread itself.
- **Reaching, leaning, sitting** — *reaching for*, *lean into*, *worth sitting
  with*, *keep coming back to*, *where I landed*. Write what was done or
  decided.
- **The tell, the unlock, the shift** — *that's the tell*, *the unlock is*,
  *the shift is*. State the fact.

Vicky's own figurative words survive this list. *Power-scale*, *dip*,
*grinding*, *rabbit hole*, *final nail in the coffin*, *dirt cheap* and the
house voice's *crucible* are hers. Her figures are common idioms a person says
out loud. Claude's are abstract nouns made to act. The test is whether the
metaphor came from her record or from you.

## Claude's own framing

- **Worth** — *worth noting, worth asking, worth a look, worth considering*.
  Cut it and say the thing. *Why it is worth it* to explain a trade-off stays.
- **Announced importance** — *this matters*, *here's the useful part*, *the
  right question*, *the most interesting part*, *the one that surprised me
  most*, *if I had to pick one*. Drop the label and state the point.
- **Totalizing** — *that's the whole game*, *the only thing that matters*,
  *the entire point*. Scale it to what is true.
- **Honesty tags** — *honestly*, *to be honest*, *the honest version*, *I want
  to be careful here*. Cut them. Everything is meant honestly.
- **Quietly** — *quietly removes a step*, *what they quietly refuse*. Cut it
  unless something was really done without notice.
- **Drama words** — *settled* and *unsettled* for a decision, *wrecked* or
  *shattered* for a measured effect, *the reckoning will come*. Use the
  measured word: *open*, *decided*, *slowed by 20%*.
- **Consultant talk** — *pressure-test, north star, right-size, compounds,
  unpack, double-click, at the end of the day, lessons learned*. Use the plain
  verb.

## Shape of the piece

- **Formula openers** — *In today's fast-paced world*, *In the ever-evolving
  landscape of*, *When it comes to*, *At its core*, *Picture this*, *As a
  player, you know*. Open with the content.
- **Formula endings and joins** — *Despite its X, Y faces challenges*; *In
  conclusion*; *Overall*; *Ultimately*; *In essence*; *Furthermore*;
  *Moreover*; *Additionally*; *That being said*; *It is important to note*.
  Stop on the last real point, and join with what actually happened: *but
  then*, *the problem was*, *so I tried something else*. Often no join is
  needed.
- **Hook lines** — *But here's the kicker*, *Here's the thing*, *Here's where
  it gets interesting*, *That's only half the story*, *Real talk*, *Why does
  this matter?* followed by its own answer. Say the next fact.
- **Talking about the writing** — *In this article we will explore*, *Let's
  dive in*, *Let's break it down*, *I'm going to make three points*, *I hope
  this helps*, *what I'd leave you with*, notes about your training or cutoff
  date. Open with the content and stop when it ends.
- **Tidy progression** — three attempts rewritten as one inevitable path. Keep
  the order it happened in: what was tried, what broke, what replaced it.
- **Even rhythm** — every paragraph three sentences, every sentence the same
  length. Break it up. A short one ends a point well. Leave a deliberate
  fragment alone. Four or more short sentences in a row is the opposite tell;
  join some with *and*, *but*, *which means that*.
- **Symmetry** — every section the same size, both sides of an argument given
  equal room, a summary at the end of each. Let the part the author cares about
  run long and the rest stay short.
- **Quotable everything** — a metaphor or punchline in every paragraph, or a
  one-line thesis restated at the end. One good phrase beats five attempts at
  wit. Most sentences exist to get the reader from A to B, and that is their
  whole job.
- **Placeholders** — leftover *[citation needed]*, *[TODO]*, bracketed names,
  citation debris like *turn0search0* or *[cite: 1]*. Fill them or drop the
  sentence.

## Formatting

- **Bold on key terms** — emphasis sprinkled over every important noun. Let the
  sentence do the emphasis; keep bold for the rare word that must not be
  missed.
- **Title Case Headings** — use sentence case, unless the surrounding document
  does otherwise. Her dev blog uses Title Case and jokey headings (*Hit Points
  With Feelings*), so a blog post follows the blog.
- **Inline-header bullets** — a wall of `- **Term**: one line of description`
  where the ideas connect. Write the paragraph; keep bullets for genuinely
  parallel, unconnected items.
- **Numbered everything** — sections numbered for the sake of it, *first /
  second / third* holding up a plain sequence, *three things to know*, *key
  takeaways*. Structure supports the argument; it is not the personality of
  the piece.
- **Tables and emoji as decoration** — tables for prose, ⭐ and 🎯 as headings.
  Tables hold data with real columns; emoji stay out unless the house style
  uses them.
- **Em dash flood** and **horizontal rules before every heading** — commas,
  periods, and the heading alone do the job. She uses a spaced dash pair for
  one aside in a paragraph, ` — like this — `, and that stays. Three in a
  paragraph is the tell.
- **Curly quotes and stray Markdown** — straight quotes, and formatting that
  matches the target format (Markdown, wikitext, plain text, code comment).
