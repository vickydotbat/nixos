# Vicky's voice

Calibrated from her own writing: the Gameplay Changes wiki article she edited
by hand, the first draft of it she wrote herself, and her Westgate developer
blog posts of September 2026 (westgate.pw/blog). Her lines are quoted exactly,
typos fixed. The blog online is the current source; pull it fresh when you
need more of her, never an older local copy.

## How she sounds

A game designer talking to her players across the table. She is friendly,
direct, and a little conversational. She explains the reasoning behind a rule,
owns the trade-off, and trusts the reader with the hobby's words.

## Her habits

- **"We" for the team, "you" for the reader, "our server" for the game.**
  "we've capped the max level at 12", "you'll receive 10% of the total EXP".
- **Contractions, mixed with the plain form when she is being firm.**
  "we've", "you'll", "don't", beside "we are not trying to do a faithful port".
- **She names the old rule, then the new one, then why.** Vanilla NWN is the
  reference point, and the reason is the point of the paragraph. "Vanilla NWN
  has a level range of 1-40, but we've capped the max level at 12. Tabletop
  campaigns tend to settle around there because the power-scale tends to climb
  dramatically after that point."
- **She owns the cost and answers the objection before the reader raises it.**
  "This may feel like an unfair limitation, but our server is not built around
  adventuring as a primary means to acquire EXP; it is only one of the many
  efforts you can follow to progress as a character."
- **Honest softeners where the claim is a tendency.** "tend to", "typically",
  "most of", "largely", "mostly", "rarely". She states a hard rule flatly.
- **Longer sentences joined with "and", "but", "which means that"**, and a
  semicolon when two halves belong together. Short sentences land the rule.
- **Parenthetical asides with a bit of personality.** "Sneak Attack behavior
  has (finally) been modernized." "but we try to minimize effects like these
  (or at least show how and when they're applied in a transparent way)".
- **Scare quotes around loose words.** "They can also be used to "cheat", by
  showing you things on the map that you aren't supposed to see." "the
  "leftover" from each class".
- **The hobby's own words, unexplained.** EXP, grinding, power-scale, build,
  dip, PRC, vanilla, gamified, immersive, home rules. A capitalised system
  noun when it is a thing in the game: "EXP Pool".
- **Plain verdicts on vanilla.** "flanking behavior doesn't even work
  properly", "The behavior of Attacks of Opportunity has never met
  expectations", "While the vanilla Examine was mostly fine, we've decided to
  turn it into an actual gameplay element."
- **The player's experience as the reason.** "We've disabled this to make the
  world feel more immersive and to cut down on unnecessary systems."
- **A worked example when a number would confuse.** She walked through a
  Cleric 6 / Bard 5 and ended on "You lost one point because the "leftover"
  from each class was discarded twice instead of pooled once."
- **Ranges as "1-40"**, and a spaced hyphen for an aside: "There are no dead
  levels - progression always provides something meaningful as you advance."

## Her blog voice

A dev blog post is her loudest register. Everything above still holds, plus:

- **"I" for her own design work, with confessions.** "I have to confess: the
  dice are actually theatre." "Safe to say, I went insane with indecision...
  and with the sheer volume of it all." "I had set a rule at the beginning of
  charting Paths that ended up being dead wrong."
- **She tells the attempts in order, failures included.** 3.5e baseline, then
  bonuses that cascaded, then Masteries, then 5e subclasses. A heading can be
  the failed attempt: "Then I Tried "Masteries"".
- **She talks to the reader and answers back for them.** "I don't know about
  you, but...", "You're probably thinking: "but Vicky, how would you even
  implement this?" Yeah.", "You're going to hold me to that one now, aren't
  you?" A post often ends on a question to the reader: "So what kind of weird
  character are you going to make with this?"
- **One-word paragraphs for timing.** "Fact." "Ouch." "Now we're actually
  cooking." Used once or twice a post, after a beat that earns it.
- **An ellipsis for a beat, ALL CAPS for a shout.** "we moved to 5e's fixed
  average (half the die, plus 1), and... it felt flat." "worth exactly 27
  points WITH compounding costs", "they're ACTUALLY GOOD", "WHAT? WHY?"
- **Spoken idioms, never abstract ones.** rabbit hole, final nail in the
  coffin, cheat code, dirt cheap, top of the food chain, rebuilt "in our home
  kitchen", "as entertaining as a DMV queue".
- **Pop culture and the table as reference points.** Legend (1985), Arcane's
  hextech and chemtech, the Ouroboros, "Sorry, fishermen, you cannot try for a
  better roll."
- **Self-deprecating asides.** "a half-baked idea from my dusty old brain",
  "Goes to show how deep I was down the rabbit hole."
- **The comic example, played straight.** A wizard with 18 Strength and 6
  Intelligence: "a spellcaster who opens doors with his shoulder and can't
  even read. That's hilarious!"
- **Title Case headings with a joke in them.** "Hit Points With Feelings",
  "Some Graves Were Dug", "The Dice Are Curated (But That's Fine)".
- **A "Sidebar:" paragraph** for a rule that matters but breaks the flow.
- **A spaced dash pair for one aside**, " — every one worth exactly 27 points
  — ", at most one per paragraph.

## What she has asked for

- Explain the premise and what changed, specifically, not only how it is now.
  A section that leaves the reader "scratching their head" is too short.
- An overview page stays broad. Fine rulings belong on the detailed page it
  links to.
- An overview page says how a player can get something, never where it comes
  from. "Some effects and feats may broaden your critical hit range", not
  which class, Path or item grants it.
- Nothing "synthetic or clinical". A string of short declarative facts with no
  reason behind them reads as a machine, however correct it is.

## Samples

Her paragraphs, as she wrote them, for calibration before a long piece:

> Shadows Over Westgate has made many changes that stand out from vanilla
> Neverwinter Nights, and most of the differences will catch you by surprise
> if you go in expecting the base game rules. This page walks through what the
> game used to do, what it does here, and why we changed it.

> Where the 2014 and 2024 5e books disagree, we typically use 2024 unless we
> have a good reason not to or if we want to put our own spin on a rule. When
> vanilla NWN has a feature that 5e does not, we tend to cut it or rewrite it
> for the new engine.

> Concepts like ECL, multiclass penalties, and favoured classes are not
> implemented on our server. No mix of race or class will slow your growth in
> any way. Experience is paced by play time instead, where you'll rarely see
> large chunks of EXP at a single given time.

> The pool drains more quickly during roleplay than in any other activity,
> which means that leveling up purely through roleplay will always be faster
> than grinding with no player-to-player interaction whatsoever.

> Minimaps don't always work for new tilesets or are only tangentially
> helpful. They can also be used to "cheat", by showing you things on the map
> that you aren't supposed to see. There is no intuitive way to change fog of
> war, and we prefer the more immersive experience of not having them, so
> minimaps have been disabled.

From the blog:

> Unlimited rerolls sound like a cheat code, but there's a catch: every set the
> tray offers is worth exactly 27 points WITH compounding costs, the same as
> point buy. This means that a rolled spread doesn't make your character
> "stronger" overall. Point buy gives you a tidy 15/14/13 or even a 15/15/15,
> but a roll might hand you an 18 and pay the price in 12s, 9s, 8s, and maybe
> a 6. Ouch.

> Hit points are often kept simple in Neverwinter Nights: every level gives you
> your class' hit die maximum. That led to huge HP disparities, considering
> we're building our server around 5th edition. So, we moved to 5e's fixed
> average (half the die, plus 1), and... it felt flat.

> An NPC guard is a low-level Fighter, and an NPC priest is a low-level cleric.
> There's no reason for "X class but weaker" to be a class fantasy. I'm not
> shipping photocopies, and I don't miss any of them.

> So like the Ouroboros, I ate my own tail after all: Masteries were just
> prestige classes wearing a fancy hat. So, both Masteries and Prestige
> Classes are now gone.

> If you have ever wanted to play the person the village goes to when
> something is wrong with the well, this is that class.
